# 4TU real-world utility benchmark

Activity-level scoring of Subterra's detector against trial-trench ground truth
from 125 real utility surveys in the Netherlands. Generated by
`scripts/score_4tu_benchmark.py` into `artifacts/4tu/benchmark.json`.

> **Scope.** These results are **activity-level only**. No candidate is matched
> to a utility, and no positional or depth accuracy is measured. An unmatched
> detector response is **not necessarily a false alarm**, because the trial
> trench covers only part of the surveyed ground.

**The headline is a null result: the detector's candidate density carries no
usable information about whether a trench found utilities, or how many.**

## 1. What the truth actually is

`Codebook.pdf` defines the field this rests on, verbatim:

> **Amount of utilities** — *The number of utilities found. Integer value.*

"Found" means **found in the trial trench**. That makes a reported `0` a
positive statement that the trench found nothing — not a blank — which is what
makes any negative measurement possible here at all.

| population | activities |
|---|---|
| trench found ≥1 utility | **112** |
| trench found **0** — attested empty | **7** |
| field blank — **unrecorded, not zero** | 6 |
| **total** | **125** |

The join to detector output is by `LocationID` and covers **125 of 125**.

### A correction to `4tu-characterisation.md`

That document stated *"4TU contains no control or background activity … A
false-alarm rate cannot be measured from it."* The first half is **too strong**
and is corrected here: no activity was *chosen* as background, but **7 trenches
were dug and found nothing**, and the source says so numerically. The
conclusion is unchanged — 7 is too few for a rate — but the reason is "the
negative population is small", not "there is no negative population".

The earlier document was reasoning about the blank `Utility discipline` field.
`Amount of utilities` is a different field with a different meaning, and the
distinction was missed.

## 2. The limitation that bounds every number below

**A trial trench is a small excavation inside a much larger surveyed area.**

A utility lying under a survey line but outside the trench is absent from the
truth and present in the ground. So on this corpus an unmatched detector
response **cannot be called a false positive** — it may be a real utility the
trench never reached.

That is why the measure reported for zero-utility activities is an
**unexplained response rate**, not a false-alarm rate, and why the field is
named that way in code rather than only described that way in prose.

There are also **no trench coordinates**: the publisher removed geospatial
information to protect utility locations. Object-level scoring is therefore
blocked in code, not merely omitted.

## 3. Results

Candidate density, per 1,000 traces, at the detector's published defaults
(threshold 3.0, min_cells 3). **No parameter was changed for this benchmark.**

| group | activities | median | 
|---|---|---|
| trench found ≥1 utility | 112 | **31.18** |
| trench found 0 | 7 | **24.34** |

**Separation (AUC): 0.445.** This is the probability that a randomly chosen
utility-bearing activity shows a higher candidate density than a randomly
chosen trench-empty one. **0.5 means no separation.** 0.445 is no separation,
very slightly reversed.

**Count agreement (Spearman ρ): −0.062**, over 112 activities. There is **no
monotonic relationship** between how many utilities a trench found and how many
candidates the detector produced.

Note the two measures point slightly different ways — the medians favour the
expected direction, the rank-based AUC does not. With only 7 activities in the
zero group, that inconsistency is exactly what "no reliable separation" looks
like, and neither figure is quoted without the other.

**Activity-level response rate: 1.00.** The detector produces at least one
candidate in every utility-bearing activity — and also in every trench-empty
one. This number is reported for completeness and **is not evidence of skill**;
there is no threshold at which the detector is silent, so it cannot
discriminate.

**Unexplained response rate: not computed.** Only 7 activities have an attested
zero, below the 10 required before a rate is reported. The raw counts are given
instead.

## 4. Reading this honestly

Two readings are available and the data does not separate them:

1. **The detector does not discriminate.** Consistent with the BAM benchmark
   (recall 0.065–0.093) and with the ring z-score's measured width saturation:
   a buried utility is a broad, laterally coherent target, which is the case
   the statistic is structurally weakest on.
2. **The truth is incomplete in a way that erases the signal.** If trench-empty
   activities routinely contain utilities outside the trench, the "negative"
   group is not negative and no method could separate the groups.

Both are plausible. Reading (1) is better supported, because the same detector
also scored poorly on BAM where the truth *is* complete for the specimen — but
that is a controlled concrete specimen, and the inference across corpora is
weak. **Nothing here should be reported as a measured false-alarm rate on
real-world ground.**

**No threshold was tuned in response to these results.** Tuning against truth
this thin would be fitting noise.

## 5. What is verified / documented / unavailable

**Verified from files** — 125 activities parsed; the 112/7/6 split; complete
125-of-125 join to detector output; candidate counts and trace counts per
activity.

**Declared by source** — the utility counts themselves, and the codebook
definition of the field. Disciplines, materials and diameters are published but
are only partly populated (43/125 materials, 60/125 diameters) and are not
scored.

**Not available** — trench coordinates; trench extents; per-utility depth in a
usable form; any basis for matching a candidate to a utility; a negative
population large enough for a rate.

## 6. Gates

`benchmark.gates` holds `OBJECT_LEVEL_STATUS = BLOCKED`, reason *"4TU publishes
no trench coordinates, so no candidate can be matched to a utility"*.
`require_object_level_evidence()` raises `ObjectLevelBlocked`;
`benchmark.fourtu_scoring.score_object_level` refuses for the same reason.
Activity-level scoring never calls the gate.

| id | blocks | resolution |
|---|---|---|
| `trench-coordinates` | per-object precision/recall, IoU, distance, positional F1 | author contact; a confidentiality decision, not an omission |
| `trench-is-a-subset-of-the-survey` | calling an unmatched response a false positive | trench extents, which are not published |
| `attested-zero-population-is-small` | a false-alarm rate on real-world ground | more zero-utility trenches, or another corpus |

The BAM gate (`absolute origin is not verified`) is separate and a test asserts
the two cannot leak into one another.

## 7. Reproducing

```bash
python scripts/score_4tu_benchmark.py --out artifacts/4tu/benchmark.json
```

Reads the existing corpus characterisation rather than reprocessing 235 million
records. The runner warns and records `join_complete: false` if any activity
fails to join — a partial join must never pass as a full one.
